## Diagnostics and imaging

- **Indonesian adaptation of a symptom questionnaire** — cross-sectional
  diagnostic-accuracy study at one tertiary gynecology clinic. 316 women
  who had already had gynecologic surgery completed the questionnaire;
  histopathology was the reference. Suspected malignancy and pregnancy
  were excluded. Score 1 at a cutoff above 16 had sensitivity 76.2%,
  specificity 59.2%, and area under the curve 0.715; positive predictive
  value 48.2% and negative predictive value 83.3%. Score 2 at a cutoff
  above 14 had sensitivity 74.3%, specificity 66.4%, and area under the
  curve 0.739; positive predictive value 52.3% and negative predictive
  value 83.8%.
  [PMID 43034794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034794/) — *why it matters:* a questionnaire in women already going to surgery,
  not a test that replaces imaging or histology. The abstract's
  background sentence of delays of up to 11 years is not a result of
  this study and is not adopted. The WHO fact-sheet range of 4–12 years
  stands. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **What clinicians say blocks imaging** — cross-sectional survey through
  FIGO committee networks, content analysis of free text. 360
  respondents, 83.3% gynecologists. Seven barrier topics: lack of
  training and expertise (173, 48.2%), cost and accessibility (98,
  27.3%), professional and institutional challenges (28, 7.8%),
  diagnostic limitations (15, 4.2%), equipment quality (11, 3.1%),
  patient barriers (9, 2.5%), and cultural and clinical resistance (4,
  1.1%). Respondents described possible effects as a general impact on
  practice (118, 32.8%), changes in surgical and medical management (84,
  23.3%), a shift in diagnostic approach (77, 21.4%), challenges and
  considerations (32, 9.7%), and improved patient-centered care (25,
  6.9%).
  [PMID 43033007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033007/) — *why it matters:* perceptions from people who answered a survey, not
  a measure of how accurate ultrasound or MRI is, and not a new
  diagnostic standard.
- **Virtual reality during endometriosis-focused ultrasound** —
  randomized trial at one tertiary centre, December 2023–August 2024.
  No sample-size calculation was done in advance. 155 women were
  randomized (80 virtual reality, 75 standard examination); four stopped
  the virtual-reality intervention. In the virtual-reality group, state
  anxiety fell from a median of 45 (IQR 33–53) to 33 (26–44)
  (P<0.0001) and heart rate from 85 (78–97) to 79 (74–89) (P=0.003);
  pain scores stayed stable. In the control group, pain rose from 3
  (0–6) to 5 (2–7) (P=0.01), without a significant anxiety change.
  Anxiety after the examination was lower with virtual reality
  (P=0.0001). Counting all 80 people allocated to virtual reality,
  86.3% reported satisfaction and 83.8% said they would use it again.
  [PMID 43050757](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050757/) — *why it matters:* one underpowered trial of a distraction method
  during a scan. The authors say it needs an adequately powered
  randomized trial. It is not a scanning protocol. Indexed in the edat
  supplement.

## Surgery

- **Right-sided bowel procedures added to left colorectal surgery** —
  retrospective cohort from a prospectively maintained database at
  IFEMEndo, Clinique Tivoli-Ducos, Bordeaux, January 2021–July 2025.
  1,058 consecutive women had left-sided colorectal surgery for deep
  disease: 898 left-sided only and 160 with a concomitant right-sided
  bowel procedure. Clavien–Dindo grade IIIb or higher within 30 days
  occurred in 22/160 (13.8%) versus 66/898 (7.3%) (p=0.007), and earlier
  in the combined group (mean day 9 ± 12 versus day 15 ± 23; p=0.034).
  Early pelvic septic complications were 16.3% versus 8.6% (p=0.003).
  After adjustment for age, body mass index, smoking, and prior
  abdominal surgery, ileocecal resection was associated with major
  morbidity (adjusted OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.13–4.21) and with early pelvic
  sepsis (adjusted OR 2.60, 95% CI 1.29–4.91). The adjusted absolute
  risk of septic complications was 8.7% (95% CI 6.6–10.9%) without a
  right-sided procedure, 16.3% (8.2–25.9%) with ileocecal resection, and
  22.0% (0.0–44.4%) with two or more right-sided procedures (n=13). The
  ileocecal association with major morbidity depended on the left-sided
  operation (interaction p=0.022): pronounced when added to conservative
  procedures, and attenuated and not significant when added to segmental
  resection.
  [PMID 43045027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43045027/) — *why it matters:* an association at one high-volume centre, not a
  trial of whether to resect the right side. The authors say some
  subgroups had few events and the result should be used cautiously in
  counseling. It does not choose an operation.
- **Quality of life six months after laparoscopic excision** —
  prospective cohort at one tertiary hospital in São Paulo, 2023–2025.
  117 women with histologically confirmed endometriosis: 41 private
  pathway and 76 public; 42 superficial and 75 ovarian and/or deep.
  SF-36 and EHP-30 were collected before surgery and at 3 and 6 months.
  At baseline, public-pathway patients reported greater pain severity
  and lower scores on several SF-36 domains; stage distribution did not
  differ significantly. Quality of life improved across most domains in
  both pathways. Pathway-by-time interactions were significant for
  several SF-36 domains (physical functioning, pain, general health,
  social functioning, role-emotional, and vitality; all P<0.05) and were
  not significant for most EHP-30 domains. Infertility-related quality
  of life did not change significantly. Superficial versus ovarian
  and/or deep disease did not separate the trajectories.
  [PMID 43050129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050129/) — *why it matters:* scores moved after surgery in one observational
  series. The abstract does not give the point change. This is not a
  comparison with no surgery, and it is not a reason to operate.
- **Bladder and ureteric excision, with urinary outcomes** — cohort from
  a prospectively maintained database at one tertiary centre, January
  2019–May 2023. 14 bladder and 37 ureteric excisions. Bladder pain
  improved in both groups (p<0.005). Voiding difficulty improved little
  or variably. After surgery, urogynaecology or urology input was
  required for 79% of the bladder group and 38% of the ureteric group.
  Visual-analogue quality of life rose from 50 (95% CI 14 to 60) to 68
  (28 to 84) after bladder excision and from 50 (46 to 60) to 80 (70 to
  80) after ureteric excision (p<0.005 for both). EQ-5D-3L
  pain/discomfort scores worsened in both groups (bladder p<0.05;
  ureteric p<0.0005). Anxiety/depression scores did not change.
  [PMID 43043415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43043415/) — *why it matters:* small groups, no comparison arm. One quality-of-life
  scale rose and the EQ-5D pain/discomfort item worsened. The authors
  say causality cannot be established. This is not a urinary-tract
  surgery protocol.
- **Three versus four robotic arms in benign gynecologic surgery** —
  secondary analysis of the PECRoP registry, nine Paris university
  hospitals, da Vinci Xi, 2023–2025. 753 women with benign or
  pre-invasive disease: 304 with three arms and 449 with four.
  Endometriosis was associated with four-arm use (adjusted OR 2.41, 95%
  CI 1.45–3.99), as were sacrocolpopexy (adjusted OR 3.21, 95% CI
  1.48–6.97) and body mass index (adjusted OR 1.05 per kg/m², 95% CI
  1.03–1.08). Adenomyosis was associated with three arms (adjusted OR
  0.60, 95% CI 0.40–0.89). Four-arm use ranged from 32% to 93% across
  centres and from 26% to 100% across surgeons. Four arms were
  associated with longer console time (+23.9 minutes, 95% CI 11.6–36.3)
  and longer operating-room occupation (+31.0 minutes, 95% CI
  15.8–46.2). Among 13 surgeons who used both configurations, console
  time was still longer with four arms (+20.9 minutes, 95% CI 6.2–35.7).
  Conversions were 2.0% versus 2.9% and major complications 2.0% versus
  2.4%. The authors say the study was underpowered for those rare
  events, and that the time difference may partly reflect unmeasured
  case complexity.
  [PMID 43055885](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43055885/) — *why it matters:* endometriosis was one reason surgeons picked a
  fourth arm. Complication rates are for the whole benign cohort, not
  an endometriosis-only comparison. This is not a reason to add an arm.
  Trial registration NCT03274050.
- **USP14 expression and symptomatic recurrence after deep-disease
  surgery** — retrospective cohort, 235 patients. High USP14 expression
  (HR 1.656, 95% CI 1.307–2.099), adhesions (HR 1.861, 95% CI
  1.013–3.419), bowel lesions (HR 2.168, 95% CI 1.278–3.679), and
  adenomyosis (HR 2.645, 95% CI 1.568–4.462) were associated with
  symptomatic recurrence. GnRH-agonist therapy of at least 6 months (HR
  0.126, 95% CI 0.044–0.363) and postoperative pregnancy (HR 0.402, 95%
  CI 0.200–0.807) were associated with lower hazards. A model that added
  USP14 to the clinical factors had a C-index of 0.896 (95% CI
  0.879–0.912), against 0.870 (95% CI 0.849–0.892) for the clinical
  factors alone. Bootstrap internal validation (2,000 resamples) gave
  optimism-corrected C-indices of 0.888 and 0.863. Integrated
  discrimination improvement was 0.044 (95% CI −0.013 to 0.11; P=0.047);
  the interval includes zero, and the authors call that result
  borderline. Continuous net reclassification improvement was 0.655
  (P=0.026). The bootstrap difference in C-index was 0.020 (95% CI
  0.002–0.048; P=0.033).
  [PMID 43050139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050139/) — *why it matters:* one centre, internal validation only. The authors
  say the model is hypothesis-generating and is not ready for clinical
  use. The GnRH-agonist hazard is not a prescription for six months of
  an agonist.
- **Single-port robot for diaphragm and rectum in one patient** — one
  symptomatic patient with six previous laparoscopic operations. A
  single 3 cm supraumbilical incision was used for full-thickness
  resection of the central right diaphragm and a mucosa-sparing rectal
  nodulectomy. Histopathology confirmed endometriosis. The authors say
  they do not know of an earlier simultaneous single-port robotic
  excision of both sites.
  [PMID 43030417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43030417/) — *why it matters:* one operation. It is not a comparison with
  multi-port surgery and not a reason to choose a robot. Indexed in the
  edat supplement. Epub 2026-10-23.
- **Rectal endometriosis read as a possible stromal tumor** — one case.
  A 45-year-old woman had eight years of difficult and more frequent
  defecation, without dysmenorrhea or heavier periods. Colonoscopy
  showed a smooth rectal protrusion. Endoscopic ultrasound suggested a
  muscular-layer lesion with intact mucosa, read as possible
  gastrointestinal stromal tumor. Laparoscopic anterior resection was
  done. Histology showed ectopic endometrial glands and stroma, with
  estrogen and progesterone receptors and stromal CD10. Symptoms
  improved. The authors also reviewed 12 reported cases and say
  intestinal endometriosis and gastrointestinal tumors can look alike,
  including when cyclical symptoms are absent.
  [PMID 43047398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43047398/) — *why it matters:* one confirmed lesion and a case review. It is not a
  rule for when to operate on a rectal mass. Indexed in the edat
  supplement.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **KRAS mutations in lesions** — systematic review and meta-analysis,
  PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD420261303629. Seven studies covered deep,
  ovarian, and superficial disease. Pooled prevalence was 48% (95% CI
  38%–58%), with I²=77%. The abstract describes mutations as most
  frequent in deep and ovarian lesions (50%–55%) and less frequent in
  superficial peritoneal disease (about 30%–35%). Variant allele
  fractions were below 20%. Associations with pain, infertility, and
  recurrence were not statistically significant. Greater disease extent
  and surgical complexity were described in KRAS-positive patients.
  [PMID 43035259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035259/) — *why it matters:* a pooled prevalence with high heterogeneity. The
  authors say the evidence is not enough for routine clinical use. KRAS
  testing is not added here. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Interleukin-11 in endometriotic fibrosis** — tissue, cyst fluid, and
  stromal cells from patients having laparoscopy, plus a public
  single-cell dataset. IL-11 in ovarian endometrioma cyst fluid was
  higher than in non-endometriotic ovarian cyst fluid (median 90.81
  versus 28.91 pg/mL, p<0.001). TGF-β raised IL11 mRNA 19.2-fold
  (p<0.05) and IL-11 secretion 3.7-fold (p<0.001). IL-11 raised CTGF
  1.4-fold (p=0.02), PAI-1 1.3-fold (p=0.027), and IL-6 1.4-fold
  (p=0.020). Collagen-gel area fell from 1.00 to 0.81 with IL-11
  (p<0.05). Neutralizing IL-11 moved TGF-β-stimulated gel area from 0.46
  to 0.77 (p=0.063).
  [PMID 43033209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033209/) — *why it matters:* a cell and fluid finding. The neutralization
  contrast was not statistically significant. IL-11 is not an OpenEndo
  candidate and not a drug. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Microbiota across 43 studies** — systematic review, PRISMA 2020,
  qualitative synthesis because designs differed. No reproducible
  taxonomic signature was found. Results varied by person, body site,
  and method. Several studies pointed at functional, metabolic, or
  immune processes, including estrogen-related metabolism, but the
  directness of that evidence varied.
  [PMID 43046779](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43046779/) — *why it matters:* the review does not name a microbiome test or a
  probiotic. Neither is adopted. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Endometriosis and migraine, shared biology** — perspective. The
  authors say the two conditions co-occur more often than chance and
  that the overlap looks like shared mechanisms rather than one disease
  causing the other. Named pathways include estrogen-dependent pain
  regulation, hormone and inflammation genetics, neuroimmune
  interaction, and peripheral and central sensitization. They describe
  shared heritability as modest. They say calcitonin gene-related
  peptide may connect hormone, immune, and pain signals, and that its
  role in endometriosis is less established.
  [PMID 43044567](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43044567/) — *why it matters:* a mechanism essay. It is not a migraine treatment
  trial and not a reason to use a calcitonin gene-related peptide drug
  for endometriosis.

## Epidemiology and burden

- **Inflammatory diagnoses, mood diagnoses, and quality of life in All
  of Us** — two cohorts built from the NIH All of Us program. A history
  of asthma, inflammatory bowel disease, or migraine was associated with
  higher odds of incident endometriosis (adjusted OR 3.06, 95% CI
  2.92–3.20). Women with those inflammatory diagnoses were recorded as
  diagnosed younger (42.9 ± 11.2 versus 44.9 ± 12.1 years). Endometriosis
  was associated with EHR-documented depression (age-adjusted OR 2.29,
  95% CI 2.17–2.41) and anxiety (age-adjusted OR 2.44, 95% CI
  2.32–2.57). Five self-reported health and quality-of-life associations
  remained after adjustment for age, race, ethnicity, and household
  income (all p<0.001). The abstract does not state how many
  participants were in either cohort.
  [PMID 43049030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049030/) — *why it matters:* recorded diagnoses in a research program, not a
  demonstration that these conditions cause endometriosis or that
  screening for them changes care. The opening "around 10%" sentence is
  the authors' background, not an All of Us prevalence.
- **What Danish patients ask of multidisciplinary care** — mixed-methods
  online survey with the FEMaLe project and the Danish Endometriosis
  Patient Association, 27 February–6 April 2025. 769 patients with
  endometriosis or adenomyosis completed it. Relevance ratings for
  referral services were 96% (95% CI 94.4–97.3) for
  gynaecological/obstetric physiotherapy, 91% (88.7–92.9) for fertility
  treatment, 91% (88.8–93.0) for a dietitian or nutritionist, 90%
  (87.7–92.0) for psychological care, and 88% (85.5–90.3) for
  exercise-based therapy. Open answers also asked for easier reliable
  information, better-informed clinicians, long-term plans, and
  follow-up.
  [PMID 43046212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43046212/) — *why it matters:* stated preferences, including adenomyosis, not a
  trial of those services and not a decision to open a national centre.
  Indexed in the edat supplement. Epub 2026-08-22.
- **Delay and fertility anxiety in one Istanbul clinic** — qualitative
  interviews with 18 women recruited from a private-hospital gynecology
  clinic. Inclusion required a previous physician diagnosis and at least
  two years between symptom onset and diagnosis. Mean age was 30.3 ± 4.8
  years. The mean self-reported interval was 7.9 ± 3.6 years. Themes
  covered normalization of severe pain, fragmented care, the meaning of
  a diagnosis, fertility worry, and the work of getting an explanation.
  [PMID 43030653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43030653/) — *why it matters:* 18 accounts from people selected because the
  interval was already at least two years. The 7.9-year mean is not a
  population delay and is not adopted. The WHO 4–12 year range stands.
  Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Stress and coping in an Italian online survey** — 446 women
  reporting a physician diagnosis of endometriosis. Mean age was 32.5 ±
  8.1 years and mean reported age at diagnosis was 26.8 ± 6.3 years.
  Perceived stress rose with the frequency of disease-related
  difficulties. Stress scores correlated with Endometriosis Impact
  Questionnaire domains, most strongly psychological (r=0.551, 95% CI
  0.483 to 0.613), social (r=0.511, 95% CI 0.439 to 0.577), and physical
  (r=0.451, 95% CI 0.374 to 0.522). Stress correlated negatively with
  positive attitude (r=−0.412, 95% CI −0.486 to −0.332) and
  problem-focused coping (r=−0.291, 95% CI −0.374 to −0.204), and
  positively with avoidance (r=0.335, 95% CI 0.250 to 0.415); all
  p<0.001. In a multivariable model, higher stress was associated with a
  higher total impact score, lower positive attitude, higher avoidance,
  and poor or fair self-rated health.
  [PMID 43044488](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43044488/) — *why it matters:* a self-selected online survey. The gap between age
  and age at diagnosis is not a delay statistic and is not adopted. The
  paper supports asking about stress. It is not a therapy.
- **Gender-diverse people navigating UK care** — interpretative
  phenomenological analysis of interviews with five gender-diverse
  people who had used endometriosis care in the UK. Two themes:
  negotiating legitimacy inside rigid systems, and the psychological
  cost of getting through care. Participants described cisnormative
  pathways, identity concealment, and self-advocacy that depended on
  energy and support. Diagnosis brought relief for some and did not end
  the need to be believed.
  [PMID 43042086](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042086/) — *why it matters:* five accounts. They are not a prevalence and not a
  service specification.
- **Endometriosis and oocyte outcomes in one ICSI dataset** —
  retrospective cohort, 14,420 injected oocytes from 1,856 autologous
  ICSI cycles, 2020–2025. Endometriosis and obesity were not associated
  with consistent reductions in laboratory or clinical outcomes.
  Overweight was associated with lower top-quality blastocyst and
  cumulative reproductive outcomes. Advanced maternal age showed the
  most consistent adverse pattern. The abstract does not state how many
  cycles were in the endometriosis group. An AI oocyte score
  (MAGENTA) was associated with fertilization (OR 1.53), blastocyst
  formation (OR 1.67), and top-quality blastocyst formation (OR 1.26)
  across the female-factor groups the authors evaluated.
  [PMID 43054110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43054110/) — *why it matters:* one laboratory's retrospective comparison. A missing
  endometriosis count means the negative sentence cannot be resized.
  The oocyte score is not a clinical test. Indexed in the edat
  supplement.
- **ChatGPT and Google answers to surgical and fertility questions** —
  cross-sectional scoring of 40 preset questions. One ChatGPT answer and
  one Google Search-linked answer were archived on 31 May 2026 and
  scored by two physicians with the source hidden. ChatGPT answers were
  longer (52.6 ± 3.9 versus 38.6 ± 4.0 words) and scored higher on
  overall clinical quality (4.91 ± 0.11 versus 4.53 ± 0.23; paired
  difference 0.38, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.45), driven by completeness
  (difference 0.94, 95% CI 0.78 to 1.10) and actionability (difference
  0.60, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.73). Safety scores were similarly high. No
  answer was judged harmful or misleading. Mean reading-grade estimates
  for both were above a sixth-grade benchmark.
  [PMID 43051543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43051543/) — *why it matters:* a scored set of 40 questions, not care. It is not a
  reason to take surgical or fertility advice from either tool.
- **Global hemostasis in infertile women with advanced disease** —
  prospective case-control study of 145 women seeking fertility
  consultation: 74 with advanced endometriosis and 71 without clinical
  or imaging evidence of it. Thrombin generation, including endogenous
  thrombin potential with and without thrombomodulin, did not differ.
  Fibrinogen, D-dimer, von Willebrand factor antigen, ADAMTS13 activity,
  and their ratio were comparable. EXTEM clotting time was longer in the
  endometriosis group and remained so after correction for multiple
  comparisons; the authors say that finding is not a prothrombotic
  profile. In a flow-based subgroup of 31, platelet-chip differences
  that were nominal did not survive correction.
  [PMID 43033801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033801/) — *why it matters:* no consistent systemic prothrombotic pattern in this
  fertility-clinic cohort. The authors say this does not rule
  hypercoagulability out in every endometriosis population. Coagulation
  testing is not added. Indexed in the edat supplement.

## Pharmacology and supportive care

- **Cochrane update: endometrioma before assisted reproduction** —
  update of a review first published in 2010. Searches of the Cochrane
  Gynaecology and Fertility register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase, and
  PsycINFO were run on 7 October 2025. Seven parallel-arm randomized
  trials, 646 participants. Two trials (173 participants) studied
  medicines and five (473) studied surgery. No trial studied combined
  medical and surgical treatment. Results were narrated rather than
  pooled because the trials differed. Every trial was at high or unclear
  risk of bias in at least two domains. Certainty was very low because
  of that bias and because the trials were small with wide intervals.
  The authors' summary is that no clear benefit was found for any
  treatment over no treatment. Live birth with a GnRH-agonist protocol
  versus control was OR 3.70 (95% CI 1.25 to 10.90; 1 study, 64
  participants; very low certainty); adverse events were not reported.
  Aspiration versus no aspiration was OR 1.66 (95% CI 0.59 to 4.62; 1
  study, 81 participants; very low certainty); adverse events were not
  reported. Cystectomy versus no cystectomy was OR 1.97 (95% CI 0.71 to
  5.49; 1 study, 73 participants; very low certainty); that study
  reported zero adverse events in each arm. No included sclerotherapy
  trial reported live birth, and adverse events were not reported.
  [PMID 43042747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042747/) — *why it matters:* the authors write that there is no high-certainty
  evidence that might change current guidance, and that plans stay
  individual, taking account of previous outcomes, ovarian reserve, and
  the patient's wishes. That limit is recorded. The GnRH-agonist odds
  ratio is one small, very-low-certainty trial. It is not a protocol to
  use an agonist, to aspirate, or to excise an endometrioma before
  assisted reproduction. OpenEndo does not state such a protocol, and
  this review does not add one.
- **Adolescents and young adults** — systematic review in Human
  Reproduction Open, not an ESHRE or NICE guideline. Six databases were
  searched from inception to May 2026. Of 14,376 records, 333 full texts
  were assessed and 23 studies (1,561 participants under 24) were
  included: two randomized trials, one single-arm interventional study,
  eleven cohorts, and nine case series. Five studies looked at hormonal
  treatment, one at another medicine, sixteen at surgery with or without
  hormones, and one at a non-drug treatment. Most reported pain
  improvement. Designs, follow-up, and outcomes differed. Most studies
  were at moderate risk of bias. Certainty was very low. Registration
  CRD420251018467.
  [PMID 43042315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042315/) — *why it matters:* the authors say the certainty is too low to draw
  inferences or to say how treatment should be tailored for this age
  group. No adolescent protocol is adopted. Indexed in the edat
  supplement.
- **Four hormone regimens and 24-month endometrioma imaging
  recurrence** — single-centre weighted retrospective cohort, Guangdong
  Women and Children Hospital. Women aged 18–45 had laparoscopic
  cystectomy from August 2015 to August 2023 and documented six-month
  cover with one regimen started within a month, without switching:
  combined oral contraceptives, a GnRH agonist, dienogest, or
  dydrogesterone. Recurrence was a new endometrioma-compatible cyst
  larger than 2 cm on at least two ultrasounds, or confirmation at
  repeat surgery or pathology. Among 1,121 women, 160 (14.3%) recurred
  within 24 months. Against combined oral contraceptives, the adjusted
  hazard ratio was 0.405 (95% CI 0.253–0.649) for a GnRH agonist and
  0.417 (95% CI 0.251–0.690) for dienogest. Dydrogesterone was 0.504
  (95% CI 0.265–0.958), and the authors say that estimate was less
  consistent in sensitivity analyses. Higher baseline CA-125 (adjusted
  HR 1.002 per 1 U/mL, 95% CI 1.001–1.004) and deep disease (adjusted HR
  2.358, 95% CI 1.593–3.490) were associated with recurrence.
  [PMID 43025488](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025488/) — *why it matters:* prescription records at one hospital, not a
  randomized comparison. The authors say the associations should not be
  extrapolated to overall disease control or to long-term pain. They are
  not adopted as a reason to choose a GnRH agonist or dienogest over a
  combined oral contraceptive. The Yselty page is unchanged; this paper
  is not about linzagolix.
- **A pain protocol during IVF, with and without endometriosis** —
  prospective single-centre cohort at Lille University Hospital, January
  2023–March 2024, with the earlier ENDALGOFIV 1 cohort as a historical
  control. ENDALGOFIV 1 had 278 patients (73 with endometriosis).
  ENDALGOFIV 2 had 186 patients (47 with endometriosis). After oocyte
  retrieval, numeric pain was higher in ENDALGOFIV 2 than in ENDALGOFIV
  1 (3.77 versus 3.12, p=0.01). That difference remained in patients
  without endometriosis (3.82 versus 3.13, p<0.01) and was not
  significant in patients with endometriosis. During stimulation,
  analgesic use was numerically higher with endometriosis than without
  (34.6% versus 17.3%, p=0.097). After retrieval, only half of patients
  reported taking the prescribed treatment.
  [PMID 43057427](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43057427/) — *why it matters:* a before-and-after comparison with low uptake of
  the prescribed analgesic. The authors say the design cannot attribute
  the difference to the protocol. The endometriosis subgroup did not
  show a significant pain difference. The protocol is not adopted.
- **Berberine in preclinical models** — systematic review, searches to 3
  August 2026. Six preclinical studies: three on endometriosis and three
  on adenomyosis; four in cells and two in animals. Five used purified
  berberine and one a multi-alkaloid fraction. No clinical study was
  found. The cellular studies reported lower proliferation or viability.
  Where it was measured, migration and invasion were lower. An
  adenomyosis mouse model reported less myometrial infiltration and less
  hyperalgesia. The authors say clinical efficacy, achievable exposure,
  reproductive safety, and hormone or microbiota effects are
  unestablished.
  [PMID 43030274](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43030274/) — *why it matters:* the review opens by noting that berberine is
  promoted online as a non-hormonal supplement. No human endometriosis
  trial is in the review. Berberine is not an OpenEndo candidate and is
  not a supplement recommendation. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Mediterranean diet, activity, and sleep against closer-age
  controls** — cross-sectional case-control study, 177 women: 77 with
  self-reported, procedure-confirmed endometriosis recruited through a
  patient association, and 100 controls from the general adult
  population. After age adjustment and correction for multiple tests,
  Mediterranean-diet adherence and physical activity did not differ.
  Body mass index was modestly lower in cases. Daytime sleepiness was
  marginally higher and not after age adjustment. Of 13 dietary items,
  only sofrito remained less often reported by cases. Menstrual quality
  of life was markedly worse in cases. Discrimination that included
  current symptoms reached an area under the curve of 0.933; restricted
  to antecedent and dietary variables it was 0.67–0.73.
  [PMID 43030256](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43030256/) — *why it matters:* diet and activity did not separate the groups once
  age and multiple testing were accounted for. The sofrito item is one
  cross-sectional finding the authors call hypothesis-generating. It is
  not a diet. Indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Mediterranean diet scores and symptom impact in Australia** —
  cross-sectional analysis, 103 women (mean age 29.7 ± 8.6 years; body
  mass index 27.1 ± 6.8 kg/m²). Mean MEDLIFE was 12.1 ± 3.2 and mean
  MEDAS was 5.6 ± 2.0, which the authors describe as moderate and
  low-to-moderate. Few participants met the screener's serve sizes:
  legumes n=14 (16.7%), fruit n=11 (13.1%), fish and seafood n=9
  (10.7%), nuts n=15 (17.9%), and olive oil n=2 (2.4%). MEDLIFE and
  MEDAS were not associated with global symptom impact on the EIQ-63.
  [PMID 43030173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43030173/) — *why it matters:* a null cross-sectional association. The authors say
  causal claims need longitudinal studies and trials. It is not a diet.
  Indexed in the edat supplement.

## Notable clinical signals

- **One Cochrane update, and it does not change current management
  guidance.** PMID 43042747 (7 randomized trials, 646 participants, very
  low certainty) found no clear benefit of medicine or surgery over no
  treatment for endometrioma before assisted reproduction. The authors
  say there is no high-certainty evidence that might change
  individualized plans. The single-trial GnRH-agonist live-birth odds
  ratio (3.70, 64 participants) is very low certainty. It is not adopted.
  There is no new ESHRE, NICE, or other guideline in this window, and no
  regulatory decision. The adolescent review (PMID 43042315; 23 studies,
  1,561 participants, very low certainty) is not itself a guideline.
- **Nothing here changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict, the Yselty
  page, the saliva-diagnostics page, or the lesion-cell ferroptosis
  sentence.** No ferroptosis, sulfasalazine, sirolimus, or MRGPRX2
  paper is in the included set. Berberine (PMID 43030274) is preclinical
  and is not a supplement. IL-11 (PMID 43033209) is not a candidate.
  Dienogest in PMID 43025488 is an observational recurrence association
  and does not move the linzagolix page.
- **Bowel surgery in 1,058 patients associated a right-sided procedure
  with more early major morbidity** (PMID 43045027; 22/160 versus
  66/898). That is one centre's practice, not a choice of operation.
  Quality-of-life scores improved across most domains by 6 months in 117
  women (PMID 43050129) without a non-surgical comparison. Urinary-tract
  excision in 51 patients (PMID 43043415) improved one pain measure and
  worsened EQ-5D pain/discomfort. A fourth robotic arm was used more
  often when endometriosis was present (PMID 43055885; adjusted OR 2.41)
  and took longer; complication rates were not an endometriosis-only
  result.
- **The postoperative hormone comparison is not a regimen to copy.**
  Among 1,121 women with six months of one prescription, GnRH-agonist
  and dienogest hazards of imaging recurrence were lower than combined
  oral contraceptives (PMID 43025488). The authors say not to
  extrapolate to pain or overall control. A separate prediction model
  (PMID 43050139) also reports a low hazard for six months of a GnRH
  agonist and says the model is not for clinical use. Neither paper
  sets a hormone duration.
- **Two non-invasive ideas are not standards.** The Chapron
  questionnaire (PMID 43034794; AUC 0.715 and 0.739) was tested in women
  who had surgery. Its "up to 11 years" sentence is background and is
  not adopted. The WHO 4–12 year range stands. A FIGO-network survey
  (PMID 43033007; 360 respondents) describes training and cost as the
  barriers clinicians named. It does not measure imaging accuracy.
  Virtual reality during ultrasound (PMID 43050757) lowered anxiety in
  an underpowered trial and is not a protocol.
- **Delay figures in this window are not population estimates.** The
  Istanbul interviews (PMID 43030653) averaged 7.9 years in 18 women
  selected for an interval of at least two years. The Italian survey's
  ages (PMID 43044488; 446 women) are not a delay study. An adenomyosis
  review that says diagnosis is delayed by about 11 years (PMID
  43027998) is about adenomyosis, was dropped, and that figure is not
  used for endometriosis. The WHO 4–12 year range stands.
- **Coagulation testing is still not a work-up.** Global hemostasis
  assays in 145 fertility-clinic patients did not show a consistent
  prothrombotic pattern (PMID 43033801). KRAS mutation prevalence was
  pooled at 48% with I²=77% (PMID 43035259) and is not a clinical test.
  The microbiota review (PMID 43046779; 43 studies) found no reproducible
  signature and is not a probiotic. Two Mediterranean-diet analyses
  (PMID 43030256, n=177; PMID 43030173, n=103) did not support a diet
  recommendation.
- **Patient-experience papers do not set a service.** Danish preference
  ratings (PMID 43046212; 769 people with endometriosis or adenomyosis)
  are not evidence that physiotherapy, dietetics, or psychology change
  outcomes. Five UK gender-diverse interviews (PMID 43042086) are not a
  pathway. ChatGPT scored higher than one Google-linked answer on 40
  questions (PMID 43051543) and is not a source of surgical or fertility
  decisions. The IVF pain protocol (PMID 43057427) did not show a
  significant pain difference in the endometriosis subgroup.
- **The salivary-microRNA letter was not readable.** PMID 43038747 has
  no abstract, and Europe PMC did not provide open full text. Its title
  was not turned into a finding. The saliva-diagnostics page is
  unchanged because this record supplies no accuracy figure.
- **Two single operations are not a technique standard.** One
  single-port robotic diaphragm and rectal excision (PMID 43030417) and
  one rectal lesion mistaken for a stromal tumor (PMID 43047398) are
  cases. The migraine perspective (PMID 43044567) does not establish a
  calcitonin gene-related peptide treatment for endometriosis.
