## Diagnostics and imaging

- **SONAZOID four-dimensional salpingography in endometriosis-related infertility** —
  retrospective series of 100 patients (200 fallopian tubes). Tubes were
  grouped by degree of patency or obstruction. Peak injection pressure and
  pain were higher with bilateral obstruction than in the other groups
  (p<0.05); the abstract says the significant comparisons were mostly large
  (Hedges' g > 0.8). Hysteroscopy with laparoscopy was the reference.
  Diagnostic accuracy of 4D-HyCoSy against that reference is reported as
  95.23% (20/21).
  [PMID 42978179](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978179/) — *why it matters:* a selected tubal-patency series, not a test that replaces
  laparoscopy and not a diagnosis of endometriosis itself. The 20/21 figure
  is the comparison the abstract reports; it is not an accuracy for all 200
  tubes.
- **68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT in eight women** — fibroblast activation protein is
  described as strongly expressed in perilesional stroma. Eight women had
  the scan for cyclic pelvic or extrapelvic pain and solid nodular lesions
  that were later confirmed as endometriosis on histopathology. The abstract
  says the foci were shown with high accuracy compared with other imaging
  and does not report a sensitivity, specificity, or AUC. The authors cite
  a 4–11 year diagnostic delay as the reason to look for new imaging.
  [PMID 42640595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42640595/) — *why it matters:* eight confirmed cases are not a diagnostic standard. The
  delay sentence is the authors' and is not adopted. This repo keeps the
  WHO fact-sheet range of 4–12 years.
- **Unpaired multi-modal classifier for two imaging signs** — EndoFusion
  detects pouch-of-Douglas obliteration and bowel nodules from transvaginal
  ultrasound or MRI when a patient has only one of those scans. On the
  authors' dataset, single-modality inference had an average AUC of 0.827
  (95% CI 0.790–0.861).
  [PMID 42594747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42594747/) — *why it matters:* the authors call it an initial proof of concept. It is
  not a test to use, and it does not speak to superficial disease.
- **Decidualized ovarian endometriomas in pregnancy** — systematic review,
  13 studies, 35 cases. The described ultrasound sequence was a simple cyst
  with little vascularity in early pregnancy, vascularized papillary
  projections in the second trimester, and a solid-appearing highly
  vascularized mass in the third. Most cases were managed surgically.
  Expectant management, in the reports that used it, was followed by
  postpartum regression and no reported complications. No malignant
  transformation was identified in the included cases.
  [PMID 42570719](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42570719/) — *why it matters:* a proposed pattern from 35 cases, not a rule to watch
  every pregnancy mass. It does not change the need for histology when a
  mass is suspicious.
- **BDNF as a candidate marker** — narrative review of brain-derived
  neurotrophic factor in gynecological cancers and endometriosis. Searches
  ran through June 2025. In endometriosis the authors associate higher
  circulating and tissue BDNF with neuroangiogenesis, pelvic pain, lesion
  severity, and inflammatory remodeling, and they say the associations are
  heterogeneous. They conclude that diagnostic and monitoring use has not
  been established.
  [PMID 42982216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982216/) — *why it matters:* a candidate, not a blood test. No pooled accuracy is
  reported.
- **Adolescent Herlyn–Werner–Wunderlich syndrome with an endometrioma** —
  one radiology case. A 16-year-old with 1.5 years of progressive
  dysmenorrhea had uterus didelphys, a left adnexal cyst read as an
  endometrioma, and left renal agenesis on ultrasound. MRI showed a
  noncommunicating left uterine unit with cervicovaginal atresia and
  hematometra (type 1.2) and a left ovarian endometrioma. CT urography
  confirmed absent left kidney and ureter.
  [PMID 42633389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42633389/) — *why it matters:* one obstructed anomaly found because of pain. It is not
  a screening pathway. Imaging of the anomaly is the point of the report;
  the endometrioma is the associated finding.

## Surgery

- **Quality of life after surgery, across #ENZIAN compartments** —
  prospective multicenter cohort at certified centres in Austria, Germany,
  and Switzerland (NCT05624567). Women aged 18–50 with surgically and
  histologically confirmed endometriosis who completed a baseline EHP-30
  were included: 627 at baseline, 430 at 6 months, and 325 at 12 months.
  EHP-30 improved within 6 months and was stable at 12 months, including in
  deep and multifocal disease. #ENZIAN compartment and rASRM stage were not
  associated with baseline quality of life or with the recovery trajectory.
  Baseline symptom burden and complete resection were the strongest
  predictors of early improvement.
  [PMID 42983411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983411/) — *why it matters:* an observational before-after cohort, not a trial of
  surgery against no surgery. Staging did not sort who felt better. Symptom
  burden and whether resection was complete did. Epub 2026-08-07; indexed
  this week.
- **One-stage laparoscopy for multicompartment deep disease** —
  retrospective two-centre cohort (Bern and Budapest), January 2017–
  December 2025. 299 consecutive patients with pathologically confirmed
  deep endometriosis had one-stage laparoscopic resection. Clavien–Dindo
  grade ≥ II complications occurred in 35/299 (11.7%); grade ≥ IIIb in
  14/299 (4.7%); any recorded postoperative complication in 36/299 (12.0%).
  Each additional #ENZIAN compartment had an adjusted odds ratio of 1.51
  (95% CI 1.04–2.18) for clinically relevant complications. Documented
  recurrence was 43/299 (14.4%); reoperation for recurrence was 26/299
  (8.7%). Follow-up time was available for 231/299 (77.3%). Recurrence-free
  survival did not differ by bowel technique (log-rank p=0.431) or bladder
  technique (p=0.116).
  [PMID 42972913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42972913/) — *why it matters:* extent of disease tracked complications in this series.
  It is not a comparison with staged surgery, and it is not a recurrence
  rate for everyone with deep disease.
- **Hysterectomy and whether the ovaries were removed** — population-based
  retrospective cohort of 990 people aged 19–50 with pathologically
  confirmed endometriosis who had a hysterectomy in 2001–2016. Reoperation
  was 19.2% with ovarian conservation, 13.2% with unilateral
  salpingo-oophorectomy (adjusted HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.36–0.88), and 4.9% with
  bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (adjusted HR 0.20; 95% CI 0.12–0.35).
  The common reasons were adhesions, endometriosis recurrence, and
  endometrioma. Physician visits and fills for opioids, oral contraceptives,
  and endometriosis medicines at 3–12 months did not differ between ovarian
  conservation and bilateral oophorectomy. Hormone therapy was filled for
  67.2% of those who entered premature surgical menopause.
  [PMID 42962755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42962755/) — *why it matters:* fewer later operations is not the same as less pain
  care. This is not a reason to remove ovaries. The pain-medicine result
  is the limit on that reading.
- **Laparoscopic decompression for endometriosis-related sciatic pain** —
  systematic review and meta-analysis, PROSPERO CRD420251107880, studies
  from 2000 to 2025. Eight studies, 744 people. Preoperative sciatica
  attributed to endometriosis was reported in 619/744 (83.2%). Operative
  findings included sciatic-nerve involvement in 389 (52.3%) and sacral
  nerve-root involvement in 335 (45.0%). Across five studies with
  extractable pain scores (n=520), the pooled mean reduction was 6.47
  points (95% CI 5.55–7.43) on 10-point scales, at least 3 months after
  surgery. Major complications occurred in 8% and were mainly tied to
  other pelvic procedures. The authors rate the evidence as mostly
  observational with low to moderate risk of bias.
  [PMID 42200541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42200541/) — *why it matters:* a large pooled pain drop from uncontrolled series.
  There is no sham comparison here. It is not a guideline, and the authors
  say so.
- **Who operates, and whether the ovary is kept** — retrospective cohort,
  351 people aged 18–45 with pathology-confirmed endometrioma, 2012–2024,
  one tertiary centre and four regional hospitals. Ovarian-sparing
  cystectomy was done in 66%. Compared with general obstetrics and
  gynecology, minimally invasive gynecologic surgery and reproductive
  endocrinology had higher adjusted odds of ovarian-sparing surgery (aOR
  8.46, 95% CI 3.07–23.29, and aOR 8.44, 95% CI 2.71–26.27). In advanced
  disease, the same two groups more often excised extra-ovarian
  endometriosis (aOR 23.18, 95% CI 8.18–65.72, and aOR 13.09, 95% CI
  4.44–38.63).
  [PMID 41571147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41571147/) — *why it matters:* practice variation, not a trial of which specialty
  has better pain or fertility. The intervals are wide. Epub 2026-01-20;
  issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Patient-specific 3D printed models for deep-disease planning** — pilot
  randomized trial. Models came from T2-weighted MRI. Five patients had a
  model and eight did not. After seeing the model, surgeons changed the
  perceived difficulty in 5/10 responses, allotted time in 5/10, and
  estimated blood loss in 3/10. The model was referenced during surgery in
  8/10 responses. Allotted time rose from 246.4 ± 56.6 to 256.4 ± 60.5
  minutes after viewing (P=0.044); estimated blood loss fell from
  204.6 ± 154.1 to 177.3 ± 114.8 mL (P=0.003). Mean surgical time was
  271.7 ± 94.6 minutes with a model and 460 ± 225.2 minutes in controls
  (P=0.006).
  [PMID 42575434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42575434/) — *why it matters:* 13 patients and surgeon questionnaires. The control
  operations were much longer, in a sample this small. It is not a reason
  to require a printed model.
- **Insufflation pressure during gynecologic laparoscopy** — randomized
  trial, May 2024–February 2025, 147 patients (49 at 10, 12, and 15 mmHg).
  Excision of endometriosis was one of the procedures (61.2%), alongside
  adnexal surgery (64.6%), myomectomy (34.0%), and hysterectomy (25.1%).
  Maximum recovery-room pain did not differ (median 6 in each group;
  P=0.59). Surgeons called visualization adequate in 83.3% at 10 mmHg,
  97.9% at 12 mmHg, and 100% at 15 mmHg (P<0.001). Pressure was raised in
  six cases, all from 10 mmHg. Opioid use did not differ. The authors say
  the finding fits complex benign gynecologic surgery, particularly
  excision of endometriosis.
  [PMID 42409154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42409154/) — *why it matters:* a mixed laparoscopy trial, not an endometriosis-only
  trial. Lower pressure did not reduce pain in this cohort. It does not
  set a pressure rule.
- **Patient-reported outcomes after robotic bowel deep-disease resection** —
  single-centre retrospective cohort, 2018–2023. Of 92 eligible patients,
  41 completed a preoperative survey and at least one postoperative survey.
  Overall pain on a numeric rating scale moved from 5.3 ± 2.7 to 1.6 ± 1.9
  at 6 months and 2.3 ± 3.0 at 12 months (p<0.001). Ovulation, pre-period,
  period, and post-period pain each fell to a mean of about 1 at 12 months
  (p<0.001). Pain that affected work fell from 88% to under 40%
  (p<0.001). Satisfaction with bowel function rose from 39% to 63.2% at
  1 month, 80.0% at 6 months, and 69.2% at 12 months (p=0.014). Only 31.7%
  answered the 12-month survey.
  [PMID 42974830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974830/) — *why it matters:* the people who answered reported less pain. There is
  no comparison with laparoscopic resection, and most of the cohort did
  not supply a 12-month score.
- **Pain in the days and weeks after endometriosis surgery** —
  retrospective records of 155 intra-abdominal endometriosis operations at
  Skåne University Hospital Malmö, 2021–2025. More than half had a numeric
  rating above 3 on arrival in recovery; the median was 5 (IQR 0–7).
  Median opioid use was 57 mg oral morphine equivalents during surgery
  (IQR 14–90) and 9 mg in recovery (IQR 0–21). Half still needed pain
  therapy at three weeks, and 14% sought emergency care within a month for
  abdominal pain that was not a surgical complication. Pain at three weeks
  tracked preoperative pain and an in-hospital pain-service consult, not
  the recovery-room score or opioid dose. After hysterectomy, preemptive
  spinal analgesia was associated with less need for pain therapy at three
  weeks.
  [PMID 42968642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968642/) — *why it matters:* a description of one hospital's recovery, not a
  protocol. The spinal finding is a subgroup association.
- **S-shaped cold dissection for endometrioma** — pilot feasibility series.
  Twelve patients had laparoscopic cystectomy through an S-shaped incision
  at the hilum with cold dissection, intended to limit thermal injury.
  All twelve were completed without an intraoperative complication. The
  abstract says early anti-Müllerian hormone, antral follicle count, pain,
  CA-125, and ultrasound findings were compatible with short-term
  feasibility and does not report the marker values.
  [PMID 42975319](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42975319/) — *why it matters:* twelve cases can show that a technique was completed.
  They cannot show that ovarian reserve was preserved.
- **What patients would count as surgical success** — pilot survey of 36
  people with symptomatic endometriosis booked for surgery at a tertiary
  centre in Québec. 35/36 (97%) named quality of life as the main marker
  of success. There was no shared definition of failure. The mean smallest
  EHP-30 improvement they would accept was 34 points (95% CI 26.7–40.4).
  Most (91.7%) wanted a postoperative EHP-30 under 40. Expected pain-score
  reductions were 52–69%.
  [PMID 42595012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42595012/) — *why it matters:* a threshold patients stated before surgery, not an
  outcome that surgery achieved. Thirty-six people do not set the
  definition for trials.
- **Abdominal-wall endometriosis: who made the diagnosis** — retrospective
  cohort of 46 pathology-confirmed abdominal-wall cases excised 2007–2022.
  A specialized endometriosis centre was right preoperatively in 26/27
  (96.3%); general surgical teams in 8/19 (42.1%; P<0.001). Complication
  rates did not differ. In the regression model, care under a general
  surgical team had an odds ratio of 28.01 (95% CI 2.74–286.47) for
  misdiagnosis. Endometriosis ultrasound was used in 100% of the
  specialized group and 0% of the general-surgery group. Mesh became
  likely once the lesion was larger than 3 cm, and nearly certain above
  5 cm, in both groups.
  [PMID 42210872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42210872/) — *why it matters:* diagnostic accuracy differed with the team and with
  the ultrasound they used. The odds ratio is unstable. It is not a trial
  of referral.
- **Nerve-sparing excision of parametrial disease** — retrospective series
  of 29 people with advanced parametrial endometriosis. Seven had AAGL
  stage III and 22 had stage IV. Approaches were laparoscopy in 17, robot
  in 10, and conversion to laparotomy in 2. Mean operative time was
  284 ± 99.3 minutes; estimated blood loss was 201 ± 216 mL. There was one
  intraoperative and three postoperative complications. One person had
  incomplete voiding with a residual under 100 mL that resolved within a
  week. One had more frequent bowel movements that resolved within a
  month.
  [PMID 42985560](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42985560/) — *why it matters:* a single series of 29. Early urinary and bowel
  symptoms were uncommon and short in this group. That is not a rate for
  nerve-sparing everywhere. Epub 2026-11-04; indexed in the edat
  supplement.
- **vNOTES across gynecologic surgery** — narrative review of transvaginal
  natural-orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery, literature 2004 through
  June 2026. The authors say the approach is established for benign
  hysterectomy and adnexal surgery, and that endometriosis surgery is an
  emerging use. Comparative data for most procedures other than
  hysterectomy and adnexal surgery remain limited.
  [PMID 42986289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42986289/) — *why it matters:* an access technique with a thin endometriosis-specific
  evidence base. No new endometriosis outcome is reported.
- **Image-guided treatments, reviewed** — narrative review centred on
  percutaneous cryoablation. The authors say cryoablation has the strongest
  evidence for extraperitoneal and intramuscular lesions and is an
  alternative to excision there; three institutional cases illustrate it.
  Ethanol sclerotherapy is described as established for endometriomas.
  High-intensity focused ultrasound is described as promising for abdominal
  wall disease. Radiofrequency and microwave ablation are called
  investigational. The review states a mean diagnostic delay of 7–10 years
  and says randomized trials are needed before guideline use.
  [PMID 42975651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42975651/) — *why it matters:* a map of procedures, not a new trial. The 7–10 year
  delay is the review's figure and is not adopted; this repo keeps the WHO
  fact-sheet range of 4–12 years. Nothing here is a treatment to start.
- **Intestinal endometriosis-associated carcinoma mistaken for colorectal
  cancer** — one case. A 43-year-old woman with ovarian endometriosis had
  melena and a rectosigmoid tumor with uterine invasion and nodal
  metastases. The first histology, without a clear transition from
  endometriosis, was read as primary colorectal adenocarcinoma. Five years
  later a vaginal-stump recurrence was estrogen-receptor, PAX8, and CK7
  positive, and CK20 and CDX2 negative. The original tumor matched that
  pattern on review.
  [PMID 42984604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42984604/) — *why it matters:* one tumor-origin problem. Immunohistochemistry changed
  the label. It is not a surveillance schedule.
- **Pulmonary endometriosis treated with biopsy and microwave ablation** —
  one case. A 31-year-old woman with no symptoms had a right-lower-lobe
  nodule that grew from 1.8 to 2.1 cm over 3 months. Single-stage
  image-guided biopsy and microwave ablation were done. Histology was
  pulmonary endometriosis. She went home on day 1. CT at 6 and 12 months
  showed no obvious local recurrence, with no further treatment. The
  authors say safety, efficacy, and long-term results of microwave
  ablation for pulmonary endometriosis remain uncertain.
  [PMID 42978679](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978679/) — *why it matters:* one nodule. The authors' uncertainty stands. It is not
  a treatment pathway for thoracic disease.
- **Primary umbilical endometriosis** — one case from Nepal. A 38-year-old
  woman had umbilical bleeding. Clinical findings, imaging, and histology
  confirmed endometriosis. The abstract describes a discolored umbilical
  nodule with cyclical pain, swelling, and bleeding.
  [PMID 42978443](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978443/) — *why it matters:* another single extrapelvic case, in the same class as
  the umbilical case already recorded on 2026-10-30 (PMID 42947878). It is
  not a diagnostic pathway. Publication date 2025 Dec; indexed in this
  week's edat supplement.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **Estrogen, autophagy, and ECSCR in lesion endothelium** — single-cell
  and GEO analyses plus cell assays and a mouse model. Endometriosis
  lesions showed higher vascular density. Estrogen raised autophagy in
  vascular endothelial cells through estrogen receptor α. That autophagy
  lowered endothelial cell-specific chemotaxis receptor (ECSCR) and
  increased cytoskeleton formation, migration, and tube formation.
  [PMID 42988040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42988040/) — *why it matters:* a preclinical angiogenesis axis. It is not a drug
  target with a candidate, and it does not move a repurposing verdict.
- **Extracellular vesicles in PCOS and endometriosis** — narrative review
  from human, animal, and plant sources. Mesenchymal-stromal-cell vesicles
  are described as anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic in preclinical
  models. The disease-specific note for endometriosis is ginger-derived
  vesicles, which the review says reduced inflammation, angiogenesis, and
  invasion and promoted apoptosis in experimental models. Other plant
  vesicles are indirect evidence from other conditions. The authors say
  clinical use needs disease-specific studies and trials.
  [PMID 42986494](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42986494/) — *why it matters:* a review of experimental cargo, not a diet or a
  vesicle therapy. Ginger is not a treatment.
- **A tRNA fragment and MCCC1** — 3'-tRF-AlaAGC was lower in lesions and
  serum. The authors report that it binds MCCC1 (residues 383–494), limits
  p65 nuclear translocation, and reduces NF-κB-linked
  epithelial–mesenchymal transition and invasion. It reduced lesion burden
  in a mouse model. They also say serum levels predicted disease in a
  machine-learning analysis and do not report an AUC.
  [PMID 42982359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982359/) — *why it matters:* a mechanism paper with an unquantified prediction
  claim. It is not a blood test. A related letter (PMID 42980989) has no
  abstract and is not used as support.
- **Curcumin, macrophage vesicles, and angiogenesis** — in a mouse model,
  curcumin reduced lesion size, volume, adhesions, and local angiogenesis.
  In cell assays, 30 μM was required to inhibit endothelial tube formation
  directly, while 10 μM changed macrophage-derived small extracellular
  vesicles enough to inhibit tubes, including vesicles from people with
  endometriosis. The vesicles carried more miR-495-3p. Blocking that
  microRNA partly restored tube formation. BMI1 was a predicted direct
  target.
  [PMID 42976742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976742/) — *why it matters:* a mouse and cell study. The micromolar concentrations
  are assay conditions, not a dose to take. Curcumin is not an OpenEndo
  repurposing candidate, and this paper does not add one.
- **Pyruvate carboxylase and AKT** — immunohistochemistry showed higher
  pyruvate carboxylase in eutopic endometrium (P=0.022) and ectopic tissue
  (P<0.001) than in controls. Knockdown in immortalized endometriotic
  stromal cells lowered glycolytic metabolites and AKT-pathway activity;
  an AKT agonist and inhibitor were used to tie the glycolytic enzymes to
  that pathway. The inhibitors ZY-444 and octyl gallate reduced progression
  in the authors' model. A mouse allograft was used in vivo.
  [PMID 42641507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42641507/) — *why it matters:* metabolic mechanism in cells and mice. The named
  inhibitors are experimental tools, not treatments. Epub 2026-07-29;
  issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Endometrial stem and progenitor cells** — frequencies in ectopic
  lesions and paired eutopic endometrium from 72 women with endometriosis
  and 24 women without. SUSD2-positive endometrial mesenchymal stem cells
  were more frequent in eutopic endometrium of women with endometriosis.
  Those cells kept classical mesenchymal markers above 80% in eutopic
  tissue and were variable in lesions. Cells from deep infiltrating
  disease migrated more than control and paired eutopic cells. Side
  population was higher in lesions than in eutopic endometrium. Ovarian
  endometriomas had more CD146/CD140b pericyte-like cells.
  [PMID 42616347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42616347/) — *why it matters:* a cell-frequency map, not a marker to use clinically.
  Epub 2026-08-19; issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Follicular-fluid steroids during IVF** — prospective cohort, 83 women
  with endometriosis matched 1:1 to controls on age, period, stimulation,
  and serum anti-Müllerian hormone. Fourteen steroids were measured by
  liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry. Endometriosis was
  associated with higher 11-deoxycortisol and lower aldosterone and
  progesterone (adjusted P=0.024 for each). Women with endometriomas also
  had higher corticosterone, dehydroepiandrosterone, and testosterone
  (adjusted P=0.003, 0.011, and 0.003). In unilateral endometrioma, the
  affected and the opposite ovary did not differ. The authors say the
  clinical meaning is unknown.
  [PMID 42603467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42603467/) — *why it matters:* a hormonal difference in pooled follicular fluid, not
  a change to stimulation protocols. Epub 2026-07-21; issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Peritoneal adhesions** — narrative review in *Human Reproduction
  Update*, 124 studies from 2000 to February 2026. Adhesions are framed as
  dysregulated peritoneal repair (inflammation, coagulation–fibrinolysis,
  fibrosis). The endometriosis-specific claim is a peritoneal environment
  of chronic inflammation, repeated injury, menstrual debris, and iron that
  may lower the threshold for adhesions. Much of the mechanism is from
  experimental models. Prevention discussed for patients is intraoperative,
  not a drug that stops adhesions from forming without surgery. The authors
  state that no effective pharmacological therapy prevents or treats
  adhesions.
  [PMID 42600047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42600047/) — *why it matters:* a review that names an unmet need and does not fill
  it. It does not add a barrier agent or a repurposing candidate.
- **Quercetin and SERPINE1 in cell lines** — network and expression work
  on two traditional-medicine source plants, then quercetin in
  endometriosis cell lines. Quercetin reduced proliferation and SERPINE1
  expression. Cellular thermal shift and surface plasmon resonance were
  used as evidence of direct binding to SERPINE1 protein. Overexpressing
  SERPINE1 reversed the proliferation effect. Sixteen genes were carried
  forward from the network analysis. No animal or human efficacy result is
  reported.
  [PMID 42978900](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978900/) — *why it matters:* a cell-line interaction. It is not a supplement
  result and not a candidate to add. Epub 2026-10-19; indexed in the edat
  supplement.
- **Hydroxysafflor yellow A** — the main safflower-yellow constituent
  reduced viability, proliferation, migration, and invasion of endometrial
  stromal cells in a concentration-dependent way, raised cleaved caspase-3,
  and lowered PCNA, N-cadherin, and vimentin. The authors tie this to
  PPAR-γ/PTEN/Akt; silencing PPAR-γ removed the effect. A mouse model
  showed smaller lesions. No human dose or trial is reported.
  [PMID 42981628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42981628/) — *why it matters:* an experimental compound in cells and mice. It is not
  a treatment. Epub 2026-10-26; indexed in the edat supplement.
- **BCAT1 and ACLY** — BCAT1 was higher in endometriosis tissue by qPCR
  and immunoblot. In ectopic stromal cells it increased proliferation,
  colony formation, migration, and invasion, and it raised fatty-acid
  metabolism readouts. The proposed link is acetylation of ATP-citrate
  lyase. No human study is reported.
  [PMID 42974315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974315/) — *why it matters:* an in-vitro metabolism paper. It does not name a drug.
  Epub 2026-10-20; indexed in the edat supplement.
- **USP2 and mitochondrial respiration** — paired eutopic and ectopic
  stroma from 20 women with ovarian endometriosis (April 2024–April 2025).
  Ectopic stromal cells proliferated more, had lower reactive oxygen
  species, and had higher oxygen consumption, ATP, and complex IV activity
  (all P<0.01). USP2 was higher in ectopic than paired eutopic tissue.
  Knockdown in ectopic cells reversed the proliferative and respiratory
  pattern; overexpression in eutopic cells reproduced it. The uncoupler
  FCCP blunted the overexpression effects.
  [PMID 42983738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983738/) — *why it matters:* twenty paired samples and a lentiviral manipulation.
  It is a mitochondrial phenotype, not a therapeutic target with a
  candidate. Epub 2026-08-07; indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Immune-cell expression and endometriosis risk** — single-cell
  cis-eQTL Mendelian randomization against FinnGen R12 endometriosis
  (20,190 cases; 130,160 controls), 20 peripheral-blood cell types. Of
  9,157 gene–cell tests, 135 associations in 61 genes passed a
  Benjamini–Hochberg screen. Colocalization prioritized 10 genes (6
  protein-coding). LOC102723878 and RUVBL1 also passed the stricter
  Benjamini–Yekutieli bar. IGF1R in natural-killer cells was
  Benjamini–Hochberg only (PP4 0.89; OR 1.11 per standard deviation of
  expression) and was not significant in lesion expression (P=0.101). The
  authors say genetic support is not evidence of therapeutic benefit or
  reproductive safety.
  [PMID 42979209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42979209/) — *why it matters:* a priority list for experiments. IGF1R is explicitly
  not a drug claim. No repurposing verdict moves.
- **Gut taxa, immune cells, and endometriosis risk** — two-step Mendelian
  randomization across 473 microbial taxa and 731 immune traits. Reported
  associations include Actinobacteria (OR 0.76; 95% CI 0.65–0.89) and
  *Bifidobacterium angulatum* (OR 0.88; 95% CI 0.83–0.94) with lower
  endometriosis odds, and Aneurinibacillaceae with higher odds (OR 1.50;
  95% CI 1.13–1.98). Mediated fractions through named immune readouts were
  6.45%, 5.77%, and 14.30% in the two-step models. The authors' conclusion
  recommends trials of probiotics or immunomodulation.
  [PMID 42974072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974072/) — *why it matters:* genetic-instrument estimates, not a feeding study.
  The mediation percentages are model outputs. They are not a probiotic
  to start. Epub 2026-10-28; indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Gut microbiota review** — narrative review of dysbiosis, estrogen
  pathways, immune signaling, host transcriptomics, probiotics, and diet.
  *Lactobacillus* and *Bifidobacterium* are named as strains under study
  for barrier and inflammation effects. No endometriosis-specific effect
  size or trial result is reported.
  [PMID 42974266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974266/) — *why it matters:* a map of a hypothesis. It does not establish a diet
  or a probiotic. Epub 2026-10-19; indexed in the edat supplement.
- **Fusobacterium nucleatum** — systematic review, PROSPERO
  CRD420261337885, searches through 31 August 2026. Four human studies,
  384 participants at study level. Tissue results conflict: one 42-versus-42
  comparison reported Fusobacterium in 64.3% of endometriosis endometrial
  samples versus 7.1% of controls; another found no significant difference
  in *F. nucleatum* abundance (55 cases, 37 controls). Oral and vaginal
  findings were indirect. All human studies were rated serious risk of
  bias; certainty was very low. The fibrosis experiment the authors
  discuss comes mainly from one translational study and has not been
  independently repeated. The review says antimicrobial treatment is not
  established.
  [PMID 42964104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42964104/) — *why it matters:* a careful negative. Conflicting tissue studies are
  not a reason to use an antibiotic. Epub 2026-10-16; indexed in the edat
  supplement.
- **Epigenetics bibliometric map** — 1,175 articles from 2005 to 2026.
  China accounts for 442. Fudan University has 76 (6.4%). The most
  prolific author has 33 (2.8%). The mapped hotspots are DNA methylation
  (HOXA10, SF-1, PR-B, ERβ, and GATA factors), histone marks, and
  non-coding RNA. The paper does not report a new experiment.
  [PMID 42978001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42978001/) — *why it matters:* a literature count, not a mechanism result and not a
  biomarker. Epub 2026-08-03; indexed in the edat supplement.

## Epidemiology and burden

- **First diagnosis after menopause in the Nurses' Health Study II** —
  follow-up from the 1989 baseline through 2019. Among 80,148 women with
  no endometriosis diagnosis before menopause, 883 reported a first
  laparoscopically confirmed diagnosis after menopause (1.1%). Median age
  at that diagnosis was 50.6 years (48.5 after surgical menopause, 54.7
  after natural menopause). Mean time from menopause to diagnosis was 6.6
  years after surgical menopause and 5.7 years after natural menopause
  (median 4.0 years). Surgical menopause accounted for 69.9% of the
  diagnoses, and current hormone use for 69.5%. Against natural menopause
  without hormones (110 cases), natural menopause with hormones was
  similar (137 cases; HR 1.16; 95% CI 0.89–1.49). Surgical menopause was
  higher without hormones (78 cases; HR 5.79; 95% CI 4.29–7.81) and with
  hormones (477 cases; HR 3.86; 95% CI 3.08–4.82). The authors read the
  cases as a mix of previously unrecognized premenopausal disease and new
  postmenopausal lesions.
  [PMID 42887272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42887272/) — *why it matters:* a cohort count, not a hormone rule. Hormone use after
  natural menopause did not separate from non-use in this model. It does
  not change the statement, already recorded, that hormone therapy should
  follow current guidelines. Epub 2026-10-10; issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Anxious/worrier trait and endometriosis risk** — two-sample Mendelian
  randomization. The inverse-variance weighted estimate was an odds ratio
  of 3.84 (95% CI 1.42–10.41; P=0.008). The reverse analysis, endometriosis
  as the exposure for emotional indicators, was not significant (P>0.05).
  The authors say the biological path is not identified.
  [PMID 42969190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42969190/) — *why it matters:* a genetic-instrument estimate with a wide interval.
  It is not evidence that treating anxiety prevents endometriosis, and it
  is not a screening instruction.
- **Adenomyosis in Müllerian anomalies, with concomitant endometriosis** —
  systematic review, searches through October 2025. 42 studies, 135
  patients. Unicornuate uterus was 102/135 (75.6%). Where a
  non-communicating functional rudimentary horn had a reported site,
  adenomyosis was in that horn in 10/12 (83.3%). Concomitant endometriosis
  was recorded in 43/134 evaluable patients (32.1%), highest in the small
  obstructive subgroups and ipsilateral to obstruction in 9/10 reported
  obstructive cases. The authors call the evidence low quality and
  hypothesis-generating.
  [PMID 42982030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982030/) — *why it matters:* the disease under study is adenomyosis in congenital
  anomalies. The endometriosis figure is a concomitant count in 134
  published cases, not a prevalence. It does not set imaging for every
  anomaly.
- **Adiposity over time** — Tehran Lipid and Glucose Study, phases 3 to 7,
  3,159 premenopausal women aged 20–45, of whom 421 (13.3%) had diagnosed
  endometriosis. General and central adiposity indices were not associated
  with endometriosis status. The visceral adiposity index was higher by
  0.56 (95% CI 0.10–1.03; p=0.016) after adjustment for age, parity,
  smoking, education, and physical activity.
  [PMID 42489868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42489868/) — *why it matters:* one index differed by a small amount. It is not a
  weight-loss trial and not a metabolic screening rule. Epub 2026-07-23;
  issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Severe endometriosis and steatotic liver disease** — retrospective
  single-centre series, 1 January 2009–28 February 2025. Women had surgery
  with a recorded revised ASRM score and a CT within 6 months. Of 455
  patients, 232 had stage 4 disease and 223 did not. Steatotic liver
  disease (liver-to-spleen ratio < 1.0) was 48.3% versus 62.3% (P=0.002);
  severe steatosis (ratio < 0.8) was 8.2% versus 16.1% (P=0.009). Estradiol
  was higher in the stage 4 group (P<0.001). The abstract says a
  multivariate model kept the association after age, body-mass index, and
  metabolic comorbidities, and it does not report the adjusted odds ratio.
  [PMID 42249739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42249739/) — *why it matters:* an association in women who already had surgery and a
  CT. It is not a liver-screening program, and the estradiol explanation
  is the authors' reading. Epub 2026-06-06; issue date 2026 Nov.
- **Thyroid diagnoses and dyslipidemia in Japanese claims** — Japan
  Medical Data Center, women under 40 enrolled in 2023. Menstrual-related
  disorders (endometriosis and/or dysmenorrhea) were 257,897; controls were
  3,183,032. The endometriosis subgroup was 96,863. Hypothyroidism was
  recorded in 2.1% versus 0.8% (prevalence ratio 2.46; 95% CI 2.39–2.54);
  in the endometriosis subgroup the ratio was 3.11 (95% CI 2.99–3.24).
  Dyslipidemia was 5.2% versus 1.4% (prevalence ratio 3.73; 95% CI
  3.66–3.80). Age-adjusted ratios across the comparisons were 1.45–3.02,
  lower than the crude ratios. Among women with menstrual-related
  disorders, hypothyroidism was 3.4% without a low-dose
  estrogen–progestin or dienogest prescription and 1.5% with one.
  Dyslipidemia was 5.2% and 5.1%. The authors say surveillance bias, age
  structure, and confounding by indication cannot be excluded.
  [PMID 42961921](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42961921/) — *why it matters:* claims diagnoses, not a tested thyroid or lipid
  screen. Dienogest appears here only as a prescription stratum, not as a
  new effectiveness result. The real-world dienogest study already
  synthesized (PMID 42692407) is carry-over.
- **Mental disorders in male partners** — nested case-control study in a
  Japanese claims database, January 2005–July 2024. 85,478 men with a new
  mental-disorder diagnosis and 306,512 matched controls. A spouse
  diagnosis of dysmenorrhea, endometriosis, or premenstrual syndrome /
  premenstrual dysphoric disorder in the prior year had OR 1.06 (95% CI
  1.02–1.09). Endometriosis alone was OR 1.06 (95% CI 1.02–1.11).
  Dysmenorrhea was OR 1.04 (95% CI 1.00–1.09). Premenstrual diagnoses were
  OR 1.10 (95% CI 0.96–1.25).
  [PMID 42966065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42966065/) — *why it matters:* a small association in claims. It is not a mental-health
  screen for partners. Epub 2026-10-19; indexed in the edat supplement.

## Pharmacology and supportive care

- **Exercise, pelvic-floor training, and sexual function** — planned
  secondary analysis of a randomized trial (NCT05091268). Women aged 18–45
  with laparoscopically confirmed endometriosis and pelvic or genital pain
  all attended a pain-management course. The exercise group (n=41) added
  weekly supervised general exercise, including pelvic-floor muscle
  training, for 4 months. Controls (n=40) had no further intervention.
  At 4 months the Female Sexual Function Index total score was higher in
  the exercise group by 3.52 points (95% CI 0.49–6.54; p=0.023).
  Lubrication differed by 0.90 (95% CI 0.17–1.63) and orgasm by 0.81
  (95% CI 0.07–1.55). Desire, arousal, and pain did not differ at 4
  months. No domain differed at 12 months. The authors say the 4-month
  result cannot be assigned to general exercise, pelvic-floor training, or
  both, and that sexual-activity status differed between groups.
  [PMID 42483830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42483830/) — *why it matters:* a short-term difference on a sexual-function score,
  gone at 12 months, in a secondary analysis. It is not a protocol and it
  is not a substitute for treatment of the disease. Epub 2026-07-22;
  issue date 2026 Nov.

## Notable clinical signals

- **No new guideline, Cochrane update, or regulatory decision** this week.
  Nothing here changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict. Nothing here is
  adopted as a patient-facing treatment recommendation. Knowledge pages for
  Yselty and for saliva diagnostics are unchanged. The lesion-cell
  ferroptosis sentence on the public explainer is unchanged. No paper in
  the included set is a lesion-cell ferroptosis experiment.
- **First diagnosis after menopause was uncommon, and it clustered after
  surgical menopause.** In the Nurses' Health Study II, 883 of 80,148 women
  (1.1%) reported that diagnosis (PMID 42887272). Hormone use after natural
  menopause did not separate from non-use (HR 1.16; the interval includes
  1). That is not a rule to start or stop hormones.
- **Quality of life improved after surgery in a multicenter cohort, and
  #ENZIAN compartment did not sort that improvement** (PMID 42983411; 627
  at baseline). In a separate two-centre series, grade II or higher
  complications followed 35 of 299 one-stage laparoscopies, and each extra
  #ENZIAN compartment had an adjusted odds ratio of 1.51 for those
  complications (PMID 42972913). Staging tracked operative morbidity in
  that series. It did not track who reported a better EHP-30.
- **Removing ovaries at hysterectomy was associated with fewer
  reoperations and not with less pain care** (PMID 42962755; 990 people).
  Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy had an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.20 for
  reoperation against ovarian conservation. Visits and pain-medicine fills
  at 3–12 months did not differ. That is not a reason to remove ovaries.
- **Sciatic-nerve decompression** (PMID 42200541) pooled a 6.47-point drop
  on a 10-point pain scale from observational studies. Major complications
  were 8%, mostly from accompanying pelvic surgery. The authors say this
  is not practice-changing evidence.
- **Exercise plus pelvic-floor training** (PMID 42483830) raised the Female
  Sexual Function Index by 3.52 points at 4 months. The pain domain did not
  differ. Nothing differed at 12 months. A 3D-model pilot (PMID 42575434;
  5 versus 8 patients) and a mixed-gynecology pressure trial (PMID
  42409154; endometriosis excision in 61.2%) are not new operative
  standards.
- **The image-guided review (PMID 42975651) uses a 7–10 year diagnostic
  delay.** That figure is not adopted. The public explainer keeps the WHO
  fact-sheet range of 4–12 years. Cryoablation, ethanol sclerotherapy, and
  focused ultrasound in that review are not new options to adopt.
- **Mendelian randomization this week is hypothesis, not mechanism proof.**
  The anxious/worrier estimate (PMID 42969190) has a confidence interval
  from 1.42 to 10.41. The immune-cell gene list (PMID 42979209) and the
  gut-taxon estimates (PMID 42974072) are not drug or probiotic claims.
  The *Fusobacterium* review (PMID 42964104) finds the tissue evidence
  conflicting and very-low certainty, and it does not support an
  antibiotic.
- **Preclinical compounds stay preclinical.** Curcumin (PMID 42976742),
  quercetin (PMID 42978900), hydroxysafflor yellow A (PMID 42981628),
  pyruvate-carboxylase tool compounds (PMID 42641507), and ginger-derived
  vesicles in a review (PMID 42986494) are not doses, diets, or
  repurposing verdicts. Linzagolix absorption in rats and monkeys (PMID
  42895771) was already dropped on 2026-10-23 and is dropped again. The
  Yselty page is unchanged.
- **A retraction notice for a sitagliptin stromal-cell paper (PMID
  42987293) has no abstract.** The reason for retraction is not in the
  fetched record. Sitagliptin is not an OpenEndo candidate, and the
  retracted claim is not summarized.
